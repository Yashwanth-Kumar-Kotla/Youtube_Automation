## What worked
- **Top 20% (n=1): hold your pee too long.** Its features:
  - topic type: everyday body function pushed past a limit ("too long")
  - hook: question+number ("two cups" in line 2)
  - length: 40.0 s
  - ending: tease_next ("...why it can paint a fern on your skin")
  - first-frame type: not logged (see Decisions)
- The scout's sample independently shows the same premise winning elsewhere: "hold your pee" got 49.1x on another channel, and "hold your breath" got 47.4x.
  This "push past the limit" pattern is the best-supported lead we have. It is still one video on our side.
- Lightning's engagement (2-3x the like rate of the others, plus 3 comments) suggests a "surprising survivor detail" hook (the "weird souvenir" in line 2) gets people talking.
  One data point.
- Ep 1 retention was 73.84% average watched on a 41 s video (31 s average view). That is healthy for the format, but it is one video.

## What flopped
- **Bottom 20% (n=1): drink too much water.** Its features:
  - topic type: also "too much"
  - hook: question+number (E1-A)
  - length: 37.4 s
  - ending: tease_next
- The top and bottom videos therefore share the same hook pattern, premise type, length band and ending. **None of the tracked features separates them.**
  The 25% gap (1,342 vs 1,077) is within what one video's seed-audience luck produces.
- TikTok: the post that went up one minute after another got 9 views, and the space post got 150. Both came in the 11-13 burst.
  This is more likely posting behavior (burst posting, out-of-order chain) than content. The mapping is inferred.
- YouTube views are flat after the first test push. Every video lands at about 1,100-1,350 in its first day, and only pee grew after that.
  Nothing has broken out of the initial test audience yet.

## Why (hypotheses, not conclusions)
- All 4 YouTube videos getting about 1,100-1,350 views in day 1 looks like Shorts' standard seed test. Distribution beyond it depends on viewed-vs-swiped and retention, and we can't see viewed-vs-swiped.
  Until the owner reads it from Studio, we can't tell whether the hook (E1) or the payoff is what stops growth.
- Pee's late growth fits the scout's "push past the limit" pattern. Water had the same pattern and didn't grow, so the topic itself (bladder vs hydration) may matter more than the hook form.
- TikTok numbers are probably hurt by: hashtag-only captions with no hook line (ep 1's post was the only one with a real caption), the "#AutoShortss" tag, and 8 posts in about 6h.
  Untested.
