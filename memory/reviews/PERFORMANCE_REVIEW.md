## 4. What's working

Ranked by strength of evidence. "Weak" means n is small or the age confound applies; treat those as hypotheses to test, not facts.

1. **Retention is healthy across the catalog (strong).** Channel average 66.8% viewed and 56.9% stayed to watch. 8 of 24 videos hold 70%+ average viewed (Pee 77.6%, Nosebleed 77.2%, Brain Freeze 76.9%, Sneeze 75.8%, Foot Asleep 74.2%, Motion Sickness 74.0%, Space 73.5%, Sleep 73.4%). The audience that starts watching mostly finishes; the bottleneck is getting the feed to show the video, not holding viewers.
2. **Everyday-body topics dominate the top 5 (moderate).** Top 5 by views: Pee 1,842; Water 1,447; Knuckles 1,446; Tooth 1,446; Ears Pop 1,439. All five are mundane, universal body experiences (holding pee, drinking water, cracking knuckles, losing a tooth, ears popping), not rare phenomena. Median views across the 22 normal videos is 1,250; the top 5 sit 15–47% above the median.
3. **A concrete number in the hook line correlates with more views (weak, n=2 vs n=14).** The two pure question+number hooks averaged 1,644 views (Pee 1,842; Water 1,447) vs 1,186 for the 14 non-number hooks. Grouped more broadly, all 8 hooks containing a number averaged 1,342 views and 57.6% stayed, vs 1,186 views and 56.3% stayed for the 14 without a number. The views edge (+13%) is real but small, the sample is tiny, and Pee/Water are also the oldest videos — age confound applies.
4. **Myth-bust hooks earn the best like rates (weak).** Channel like rate is 1.56% (428 likes / 27,373 views). Lightning (question+mystery-souvenir, myth-adjacent) 2.5% (31/1,241), Space (question+instant-myth-bust) 2.3% (31/1,325), Knuckles (question+myth-loop) 2.0% (29/1,446). All three beat the channel rate by 28–60%.
5. **Best stayed-to-watch rates cluster on sensory, relatable topics (weak).** Nosebleed 64.2% stayed, Sneeze 62.7%, Goosebumps 62.5%, Charley Horse 62.4%, Lose Your Voice 61.8%, Cry 61.7% — all above the 56.9% channel average. These are topics the viewer has personally felt.
6. **Episode 1 converts subscribers best (weak, n=1).** "Don't Sleep for 11 Days?" gained 9 subscribers from 1,268 views (7.1 per 1,000 views) vs the channel rate of 1.8 per 1,000. Knuckles is next at 6 gained (4.1 per 1,000). No other video gained more than 4.
7. **The Shorts feed is the whole game (strong).** 92.5% of Shorts traffic comes from the Shorts feed; search is 5.3%. Optimizing for the feed (first-frame hook, swipe rate) matters ~17x more than search optimization.
8. **3/day cadence at 7:30 AM / 12:00 PM / 6:00 PM CT produced steady output with no penalty (moderate).** While publishing, the median video landed at ~1,250 views and no video was suppressed except the two Nov 16 anomalies. No spam or over-posting signal is visible in the data.

## 5. What's not working

1. **ANOMALY — the Nov 16 distribution failure (strong).** Brain Freeze: 16 views. Hiccups: 18 views. Their same-day sibling Goosebumps: 1,327 views. Every other Short on the channel has 887–1,842 views, so the two Nov 16 videos sit ~98–99% below their siblings, and both had 0 views in the last 48 hours. This is NOT a content-quality failure: Brain Freeze holds 76.9% average viewed (3rd best on the channel) but has the channel's worst swipe rate at 61.1% swiped away; Hiccups' 54.6% stayed / 45.4% swiped is near the channel average of 56.9% / 43.1%. Retention once viewed is normal-to-good, so the videos never got a seed audience. Most likely mechanism: a publish-path fault on Nov 16 (Brain Freeze published 12:00 PM, Hiccups 7:30 AM per their Insights pages) that kept them out of the Shorts feed test pool. Do not judge the Brain Freeze or Hiccups topics, hooks, or scripts by these view counts — the numbers measure distribution, not content.
2. **No breakout: the feed gives every video the same small test audience (strong).** 22 of 24 videos land between 887 and 1,842 views; the median is 1,250 and the top video (Pee, 1,842) is only 1.47x the median. No video has cleared the initial test audience into wider distribution. The channel is stuck at the seed-audience stage, not the retention stage.
3. **Engagement is near zero (strong).** 14 comments and 3 shares across 24 videos (0.58 comments and 0.13 shares per video). Only Tooth (2 shares) and Nosebleed (1 share) were ever shared. Shares are the strongest distribution signal on both Shorts and Reels, and the channel has almost none. Comments are too few to read as sentiment.
4. **Subscriber conversion is weak and churning (moderate).** 50 subscribers gained from 27,477 views, and 19 unsubscribed (net 31). 99% of viewers are new and 0% are regular viewers — there is no returning audience yet. A viewer watches one Short and never comes back.
5. **View pace collapsed when publishing stopped (strong).** Last-48-hours views are 356 (178/day) vs the 1,832/day 15-day average. The three most-watched videos right now are the newest ones (Lose Your Voice 138, Energy Drink 55, Fever 46 in the last 48 hours). The production halt since Nov 16 is directly visible in the view rate; the back catalog does not sustain views on its own.
6. **The newest batch underperforms, but the cause is unproven (weak — age confound).** The four "-guess" hooks (Lose Your Voice 1,252; Energy Drink 1,020; Chewing Gum 960; Hold Your Breath 932) averaged 1,041 views vs 1,289 for the other 17 repo hooks. They are also the four newest videos (Nov 20–21, 2–3 days live). Do not conclude guess-hooks are worse until an age-matched comparison exists. The bottom non-anomaly video, Sunburn (887 views, 48.0% stayed, 52.0% swiped), is the only clean low performer: its swipe rate is the second-worst on the channel, pointing at a weak first frame rather than topic fatigue.
7. **No length signal inside the current range — because the range was never tested (moderate).** Length bands show no views difference: 33–35s averaged 1,263 views, 36–38s 1,202, 39–44s 1,255. Average % viewed was 67.4% (33–35s), 60.8% (36–38s), 69.1% (39–44s). Every video is 33–44s long, so the catalog has never tested the 15–35s sweet spot the trend research points to. The flat result inside 33–44s is not evidence that length does not matter.
8. **Repo status is unreliable for scheduling (operational).** 10 videos marked "ready" were already Public; Blush is marked "ready" but its file does not exist in the repo. Any pipeline step that trusts repo status without a Studio check will double-post or schedule a missing file.

## 7. Directives for production

### DO MORE
1. **Keep the "What happens if/when..." question as line 1 of every script.** The format is consistent across all 24 videos and retention is healthy (66.8% average viewed, 56.9% stayed). Do not change the opening question format.
2. **Put a concrete number in line 2 of the hook.** Hooks with a number averaged 1,342 views vs 1,186 without (n=8 vs n=14); the two pure question+number hooks (Pee 1,842; Water 1,447) are the top 2 by views. Write the number into the second line, not later in the script — Knuckles buries "fifty years" in line 11 and its hook counts as numberless in this analysis.
3. **Keep topics on universal, everyday body experiences.** The top 5 (Pee 1,842; Water 1,447; Knuckles 1,446; Tooth 1,446; Ears Pop 1,439) are all things every viewer has personally experienced. Prioritize the backlog by "has the viewer felt this?" over "is this rare or shocking?"
4. **Keep the end-of-video tease of the next topic (E4 tease_next).** The series chain is intact across all 24 videos. The tease is the only mechanism in the current format that can convert a one-off viewer into a returning viewer, and the channel has 0% regular viewers.
5. **Keep lengths in the 33–35s band until the shorter test (Directive TEST 1) reports.** 33–35s videos averaged 67.4% viewed vs 60.8% for 36–38s. Do not ship 40s+ videos by default.

### STOP
6. **Stop running four "-guess" hooks in a row.** The Nov 20–21 batch (Lose Your Voice, Energy Drink, Hold Your Breath, Chewing Gum) all use question+*-guess hooks and averaged 1,041 views vs 1,289 for other repo hooks. The age confound means this is unproven — but rotating hook patterns costs nothing, and running one pattern four times in a row removes the only variable that could explain the batch. Rotate: no hook-pattern family more than twice in any 5-video run.
7. **Stop trusting repo `status` for scheduling decisions.** Verify every video against Studio Content before scheduling or posting. 10 "ready" videos were already Public; Blush is "ready" with no file. A Studio check is mandatory, not optional.
8. **Stop publishing a video whose first frame has not been checked against the hook.** Sunburn (48.0% stayed, 52.0% swiped, 887 views — lowest non-anomaly) and Brain Freeze (61.1% swiped away, worst on the channel) both failed at the first frame while holding viewers who stayed (Sunburn 58.1% viewed; Brain Freeze 76.9% viewed). The first frame must carry the hook visually, not just in the voiceover.

## 8. Trend briefing

Sources are creator guides and platform coverage published or updated in the last ~60 days unless marked otherwise. Each finding is tagged [platform fact] (YouTube/Instagram's own documentation or announcement) or [creator report] (third-party analysis of creator data).

### YouTube Shorts
- **Hook in the first 1–3 seconds; the scroll decision happens in the first 1.5 seconds.** [creator report — vidIQ, "How to Get More Views on YouTube Shorts in 2026"; PandaStudio, updated Nov 2026] The first frame must be a face mid-emotion, a contradictory claim on screen, or a counter-intuitive visual. *Adaptation for Body Files:* the first frame must show the consequence (e.g. the swollen cell, the fern-mark skin), not a title card or a calm presenter shot.
- **The two numbers that decide distribution are Viewed vs Swiped Away and Average View Duration.** [creator report — vidIQ] YouTube tests each Short on a small audience and expands only if both numbers hold. *Adaptation:* judge every new video at 48 hours on stayed % and average % viewed, not on lifetime views (see Directive 9).
- **Length sweet spot 15–35 seconds for informational Shorts; 20–35s is the tightest band.** [creator report — vidIQ (15–35s); PandaStudio (20–35s informational, 12–18s punchline)] Completion above 75% is the threshold ScaleLab sees breaking videos out of the test audience (3,000+ channels). *Adaptation:* Body Files videos are 33–44s, i.e. at or above the top of the band — run the 25–30s test (Directive 9).
- **Loop the Short: match the last frame/line to the opening.** [creator report — ScaleLab; PandaStudio] Replays are read as a distinct quality signal, and AVD above video length is the breakout marker. *Adaptation:* Directive 10.
- **Delay the payoff to the final seconds; do not resolve the premise early.** [creator report — AIR Media-Tech; ScaleLab] Viewers who get the answer in line 2 have no reason to finish. *Adaptation:* the number goes in line 2 (Directive 2) but the explanation of what it means stays at the end.
- **Burned-in captions, not platform captions: ~80% of Shorts are watched muted.** [creator report — PandaStudio] *Adaptation:* keep the current caption style; verify captions are legible on a phone at arm's length before shipping.
- **Trending-audio boosts were deprioritized through 2025; watch percentage matters regardless of soundtrack.** [creator report — PandaStudio] *Adaptation:* do not spend production time chasing trending sounds for Shorts.
- **Cadence 1–3 Shorts/day; new channels under 1K subs should post 2–3/day; space uploads 2–3 hours apart.** [creator report — FlowShorts; GhostShorts] Channels posting 2–3/day grew subscribers 2–3x faster than 1/day at equal quality. *Adaptation:* the current 3/day at 7:30 AM / 12 PM / 6 PM CT already matches this; keep it.
- **Best posting times: Friday late afternoon/evening, 4/6/7 PM, is the top window.** [creator report — Buffer 1.8M-video study, via Autoclips Nov 20 2026] Posting time mostly affects the first hours; a strong Short keeps being shown for days. *Adaptation:* Directive 14.
- **Shorts RPM is $0.07–$0.20 per 1,000 views, 3–14% of long-form RPM.** [creator report — AIR Media-Tech, 18,000 channels] Shorts ad money is thin; the channel's value is audience, not Shorts ad revenue. *Adaptation:* do not judge the channel on revenue per view; judge it on subscriber conversion (currently 1.8 per 1,000 views — the number that has to rise).
- **Undisclosed AI-generated content gets auto-labeled since May 2026.** [creator report — AIR Media-Tech] *Adaptation:* keep the existing AI disclosure practice; do not remove labels.
- **YPP thresholds double Feb 1, 2027 (10M → 20M Shorts views / 90 days).** [platform fact — YouTube announcement Aug 10 2026, via Mubert Nov 9 2026 and vidIQ] See Section 2.

### Instagram Reels
- **Completion rate is the decisive signal; saves and sends (DM shares) outweigh likes.** [creator report — Invideo Reels Guide 2026; Hootsuite; Klipor] Instagram's ranking order is approximately: watch-through rate, then saves, then sends, then re-watches. Watch-through above 40% triggers a boost; above 60% triggers viral distribution (Klipor). *Adaptation for Body Files:* the TikTok/Reels cross-posts should be judged on saves and shares, not likes. The current YouTube share count (3 total) is the metric to fix first — it is the same behavior Reels rewards.
- **Hook in the first 1–2 seconds with a big-text overlay, because Reels autoplay muted.** [creator report — Hootsuite; Klipor; Insta24] Sound-off viewers must read the topic instantly; face + eye contact in the first second slows the swipe. *Adaptation:* the Reels cut needs the hook as on-screen text in frame 1, even if the YouTube cut carries it in voiceover only.
- **Length: 15–30 seconds has the highest completion; 7–15s is most reliable for cold audiences.** [creator report — BlendPixel; Klipor; Insta24] Longer Reels (45–70s) work only for narrative/educational content that fills the time. *Adaptation:* the 25–30s test cut (Directive 9) doubles as the Reels cut — ship the same short version to Reels rather than the 33–44s YouTube version.
- **Trial Reels: test content on non-followers first; if it performs, it gets pushed to the main feed.** [platform fact — Instagram feature, via Invideo] *Adaptation:* use Trial Reels for the myth-vs-fact test (Directive 11) before it goes to the main grid.
- **Cadence 3–5 Reels/week is the sweet spot; more than daily risks burnout and quality decline.** [creator report — BlendPixel; Insta24] Best windows 9–11 AM, 2–4 PM, 7–9 PM local. *Adaptation:* the 3/day YouTube cadence does NOT transfer to Reels — post the best 3–5 of the week's Shorts to Reels, not all 21.
- **Caption first line is the hook; Instagram truncates after ~120 characters.** [creator report — Klipor] *Adaptation:* first caption line = the hook question + number, same as the video hook.
- **Trending audio still works on Reels (unlike Shorts).** [creator report — PandaStudio] *Adaptation:* Reels cuts may use trending audio; YouTube cuts should not depend on it.
- **Reels up to 3 minutes are now eligible for recommendations to non-followers.** [platform fact — Instagram, Jan 2025 change, via BlendPixel] *Adaptation:* no action — Body Files is far under the cap.
